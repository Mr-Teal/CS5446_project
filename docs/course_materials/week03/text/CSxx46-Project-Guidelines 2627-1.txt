Project Topic

The project is a core component of the course, giving you the opportunity to
explore reinforcement learning and sequential decision making in AI with an emphasis
on responsible and impactful applications. Topics should be directly relevant to the course
materials and integrate Responsible AI considerations such as fairness, transparency,
accountability, and trust.

Themes:

•   AI Assistant for X:

        Develop intelligent assistants to support decision making in speciﬁc domains, such as
        education, ﬁnance, health, well-being, elderly care, or customer service.

•   AI for Good:

        Apply AI to address broader societal challenges, such as sustainability, climate change,
        healthcare, social protection, or ESG-driven initiatives.

•   Other suggested directions:

        o   Human–AI Collaboration – systems where humans and AI jointly plan, learn, and act

        o   Agentic AI and Autonomous Decision Support – AI agents coordinating tools,
            reasoning, and actions to achieve complex goals

Project Types
You may choose one of the following project types:
1. Exposition Project on Frontiers of AI Planning and Decision Making
2. Explore a key framework, algorithm, or emerging area in AI. Possible topics include agentic
   workﬂows, PPO and its variants, reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) or AI
   feedback (RLAIF) and their variants, Chain/Tree of Thought, hierarchical or model-based RL,
   AI for scientiﬁc discovery, human–AI collaboration, neuro-inspired models (e.g., MAMBA,
   liquid        networks),      digital      twins,      or        multi-agent       systems.
   Brieﬂy explain the method, key challenges, and signiﬁcance. Include a demonstration—e.g.,
   reproducing results or running experiments. Emphasize analysis and insights into theoretical
   or practical impact.
3. Application Project
       Deﬁne and address an AI problem within a chosen theme. Apply or extend advanced
       techniques such as PPO, DQN, Actor–Critic, hierarchical planning or reinforcement
       learning, model-based RL, LLM-assisted reasoning and planning, imitation and inverse
       reinforcement learning, or strategic multi-agent decision making.
4. Competition Project
       Solve an AI task in planning, games, or robotics. Formal entry is not required, but
       demonstrate an AI controller in a suitable environment (e.g., OpenAI Gym, MiniGrid,
       PettingZoo, PyBullet, etc.). See Information Resources below.
5. Theoretical / Methodological Research (for the Brave Souls)
       Propose an original algorithm or method in planning or decision making. Possible topics
       include symbolic–RL integration, neuro-symbolic reasoning, causal inference with
       knowledge graphs, human-alignment-aware RL, scalable multi-agent coordination,
       foundation models as world models and planners, continual learning, or neuro-inspired
       planning. Focus on conceptual innovation and potential impact; implementation is
       optional but encouraged.
       o   If interested, discuss UROP or PhD opportunities with me.

Project Requirements
The project contributes 30% of the ﬁnal grade. Assessment is based on the report and
presentation, but a proposal must be submitted to receive a grade.

a. Project Proposal1
    •   Length: Maximum 1 page, single-spaced, single-column, min. 10 point fonts, 1-inch
        margins (excluding title page & references).
    •   Content: Clearly outline your plan (not detailed speciﬁcs), providing background,
        problem statement, approach, team roles, deadlines, and risk assessment.
    •   Submission: Upload via Canvas with all team members’ names, metric numbers, and
        email addresses.
    •   Grading: The proposal is not graded, but feedback will be provided.

b. Project Presentation
    •   Duration: ~15 minutes + 5 minutes Q&A (subject to change).
    •   Format: Present to the class or submit a recorded presentation if required.
c. Project Report
    •   Writing Standard: Academic, technical report.
    •   Length: Maximum 10 pages, single-spaced, single-column, min. 12 point fonts, 1-inch
        margins (excluding title page & references). All important discussions, ﬁgures, tables, and
        materials must be included. Additional details (e.g., probabilistic graphics models,
        screenshots) can be placed in an appendix.
    •   References: Use a Reference Manager (e.g., Endnote, Zotero, Mendeley) and
        follow Vancouver citation style.
    •   Submission: Upload PDF via Canvas with all team members’ details.

Audience
Your proposal, report, and presentation should be understandable to a technical
audience familiar with AI but not necessarily your speciﬁc topic (e.g., classmates).

Collaboration
    •   Clearly deﬁne team roles in both the proposal and ﬁnal report (adjustments from the
        proposal are acceptable).
    •   Ensure all team members understand the entire project.
    •   DO NOT copy-paste or merely rephrase printed/online materials.
    •   Cite all sources properly.
    •   If using ChatGPT or other LLMs, specify your contributions vs. AI-generated content. You
        won’t be penalized for AI use, but transparency is required.
