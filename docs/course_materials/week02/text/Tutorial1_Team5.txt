Problem 2
If an action has both positive and negative effects, then in the original planning problem,
the result of applying an action a to a state s is
                                        (︁         )︁
                          Result(s, a) = s − DEL(a) ∪ ADD(a)
   After removing the negative, or delete, effects, the result becomes

                               Result+ (s, a) = s ∪ ADD(a)

                              ∴ Result(s, a) ⊆ Result+ (s, a)
The relaxed result contains all the positive facts in the original result and possibly additional
facts. Since, all action preconditions and goals are positive, therefore, every plan that solves
the original problem also solves the relaxed problem. Thus, removing all negative effects
produces a relaxed problem.
    If negative preconditions or goals are allowed, removing negative effects does not neces-
sarily produce a relaxed problem. A negative effect may be required to satisfy a negative
precondition or goal. Removing that, effect could make the modified problem unsolvable
even when the original problem is solvable.
