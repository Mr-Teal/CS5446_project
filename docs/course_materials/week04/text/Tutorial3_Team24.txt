(f) Why exploration is necessary for all four methods
All four methods learn only from actions actually executed: an untried action has zero model counts (ADP), contributes no
returns (Monte Carlo), and leaves its Q-value at initialization (SARSA/Q-learning), so a purely exploiting agent can repeat a
mediocre action forever and never discover a better one. Convergence guarantees therefore require every state–action
pair to be tried infinitely often with decaying exploration (GLIE for SARSA/Q-learning; optimistic/uncertainty-based
exploration for ADP, whose model is wrong at unvisited transitions). Q-learning's        and ADP's greedy planning are
correct only if the values of all candidate actions are accurate — without trying both   and   in     , Aster cannot rank them.



(Optional) Problem 3: Reinforcement Learning in the Real World
(a) RL as a model of human behavior and decision making
Humans repeatedly face the core RL problem — choosing actions whose long-term rewards are unknown — and value-
learning models fit behavioral data well: expected rewards are updated by prediction errors (delta-rule/TD updates, often
with separate learning rates for positive and negative errors) and converted into choices via a softmax/Boltzmann rule with
an inverse-temperature parameter. Neuroimaging shows human reward-prediction-error signals consistent with TD errors,
and behavior combines model-free control (cached action values, habit-like) with model-based control (planning with a
learned transition model), with arbitration depending on uncertainty and effort. Discounting appears empirically:
intertemporal choice shows preference reversals better fit by hyperbolic than exponential discounting, and exploratory
choices correlate with novelty/uncertainty bonuses, paralleling curiosity-driven exploration in RL.


(b) Connection to neuroscience
Dopaminergic neurons in the ventral tegmental area and substantia nigra encode reward prediction errors — bursting
for better-than-expected rewards, pausing for worse-than-expected outcomes, and shifting their response from actual
reward to predictive cues as learning proceeds — exactly the TD error                             (Schultz, Dayan &
Montague, 1997). Primary rewards act as built-in        , with dopamine as a global teaching signal broadcast to action-
selection circuits, and credit for delayed rewards is assigned through learned value predictions. The connection influenced
RL in both directions: it validated TD learning as a normative model of prediction and inspired actor–critic architectures
(critic/actor mapped onto ventral/dorsal striatal loops), reward shaping, and intrinsic/curiosity-driven exploration;
conversely, RL supplied neuroscience with its computational vocabulary — value functions, policies, exploration–
exploitation trade-offs. Antecedents include Thorndike's law of effect and the Rescorla–Wagner model (precursors of the
delta rule), and Sutton & Barto's work on secondary reinforcers led directly to TD learning.
